# Dissolution Investigation
> SYNTHETIC DEMO DOCUMENT (SOP-QA-201, v1.0). Fictional content for the PharmaGuard AI demo - not a real procedure.

## Purpose and scope
This procedure describes how QA investigates a dissolution result that is below the specification limit or that is unusually low compared with historical batches of the same product. It applies to tablets and capsules. The demo specification for immediate-release products is 80% to 105% dissolved at the specified time point.

## Immediate actions
- Quarantine the batch and stop release activities until the investigation is closed.
- Confirm the result was transcribed correctly from the instrument printout and that the correct product method was used.
- Record the result, analyst, apparatus ID and test date in the investigation record.

## Phase 1 - Laboratory investigation
- Check apparatus qualification and calibration status (paddle speed, vessel geometry, temperature of the medium).
- Verify medium preparation: pH, degassing, volume and batch of reagents.
- Review the chromatographic or spectrophotometric data for integration errors.
- Interview the analyst. Retest only as allowed by the retest procedure, never to "test into compliance".

## Phase 2 - Manufacturing investigation
- Review granulation, blending, compression force, coating and drying records for the batch.
- Compare hardness, friability, disintegration and moisture with the historical batches of the same product.
- Check for equipment changes, new raw-material lots and unusual cycle times.
- Correlate low dissolution with high impurity or low assay; a combined shift often points to a formulation or process cause.

## Outcome and documentation
- Classify the root cause as laboratory error, manufacturing cause or unassigned.
- A confirmed manufacturing cause makes the batch out of specification and it must be rejected or handled by an approved reprocessing plan.
- Raise a deviation and, where systemic, a CAPA. The investigation must be closed within 30 working days.
