# Deviation Management
> SYNTHETIC DEMO DOCUMENT (SOP-QA-110, v1.0). Fictional content for the PharmaGuard AI demo - not a real procedure.

## Purpose and scope
A deviation is any departure from an approved procedure, specification or process parameter. All deviations must be recorded when detected, assessed by QA and closed before batch disposition.

## Deviation classification
- Minor: no impact on product quality, safety, identity, strength or purity; documented and trended.
- Major: potential impact on quality attributes or a repeat of a minor deviation; requires a root cause investigation.
- Critical: confirmed impact on patient safety or product quality, or data integrity concern; escalate to the Head of Quality immediately.

## Batch-level triggers
- A batch with three or more recorded deviations requires an enhanced QA review before release.
- A batch with five or more deviations is treated as high risk and needs Head of Quality approval for disposition.
- Repeated deviations on the same equipment, shift or operator group must be reviewed for a systemic cause.

## Investigation steps
- Describe what happened, when, where, and who was involved, using facts from the batch record.
- Assess impact on the batch and on other batches processed on the same equipment or in the same period.
- Identify the root cause with a structured method (5 Whys, fishbone diagram).
- Define corrections for this batch and corrective and preventive actions (CAPA) for recurrence.

## Closure and trending
- Deviations are closed when QA approves the investigation and the actions are assigned.
- Trend deviations monthly by plant, product, shift and equipment. Report adverse trends in the management review.
